Software Tools

This site is built using Gatsby. It started off as the starter boilerplate but now bares little resemblance to the original source. It is hosted on Github Pages.

Gatsby is a React-based, open source static site generator (SSG). I chose it because of its similarity to React and its support for functionality required for this site, e.g., running a blog. It heavily relies on GraphQL for data retrieval and the React-based framework isn't as light as plain HTML, but it was great to have a working site right off the bat. With a heavy amount of CSS styling, a few new React components, and a custom plugin, I feel satisfied with the product.

Newspapers

Newspapers are complex cultural artifacts composed of recipes, poems, essays, opinions, advertisements, and of course, news. They garnered popularity in the 17th century and have since been a source of information for the public. The images within them provided veracity to the happenings in the world and their puzzles have become an American pasttime.2

Nowadays, short-form content, mainstream televised media, and various forms of citizen journalism occupy many of the roles that newspapers once filled. While the form has experienced a decline in popularity in the 21st century, some publishers continue to print them while others have invested in a digital transformation to stay relevant. In 2022, the Pew Research Center reports that amid a decline in readership, newspapers have put more efforts than ever before into digital marketing.3 Their digital editions have a striking visual similarity to their printed counterparts.

Many newspaper publishers maintain a multi-column front page, and a distinct Fraktur font in their logos. These relics of the past are emblems of authenticity and reminders of their cultural value. The Washington Post and The New York Times have even introduced digital versions of crossword puzzles.

This site, while styled as a newspaper, functions more like a zine about me.

My Vision: Gatsby Meets Gatsby4

The aesthetics of this site are an intentional clash between 20th century newspapers and more modern forms of media.

I take a newspaper format that would've been familiar to the characters in The Great Gatsby and put it in tension with technology. Some interactions transition seemlessly from paper to webpage. Some don't translate as well, creating friction5 in the user experience. Some functionality might depart from tradition altogether. I leave it to the reader to determine which features fall into these respective categories.

Purpose

Analogous to printed and digital newspapers, digital portfolios and blogs are inching their way to become the new résumé. From software engineers to best-selling authors, the personal website with search engine discoverability has become popular. It's even one of the first tasks assigned to students taking an introductory technology class.

I don't deny the benefits to using a common template for a portfolio site that functions as an extended résumé. But, the goal of this site is not to be a digital curriculum vitae. Instead, it is my way of experimenting with new forms of interaction that are made possible by web system innovations. Tools, algorithms, and frameworks are constantly reshaping how readers engage with content. Newspaper design provides an excellent sandbox for exploring experiences that blend visual and textual elements to communicate dimensions of my identity.

Designing a Newspaper Vibe

The primary visual features that give my site newspaper vibes are the fonts, iconography, coloring, and columnar layouts. Of course, there are newspaper aspects that aren't added for accessibility or maintainability reasons.

Fonts

Most of the fonts used are copycats of common newspaper fonts. The main title is in UniFraktur and the primary choice for a serif font is Georgia. The sans serif font is News Cycle, which mirrors the 20th century News Gothic font. The monospace font here is for code-like text that is clearer and more familiar with the even spacing.

Iconography

There are ☞ manicules scattered around the site to draw attention to certain links. These tiny hand pointers were prominent in some 19th century newspapers to highlight particular notes, and even before that to annotate textual documents.

Coloring

This site is mostly in black and white. In the late 20th century, some newspapers started printing in color. You might find color on some pages, because I love color. There is also a dark mode that can be toggled using a button in the site footer.

Layout

The main title is at the top of every page, as well as sticky, clickable headings for navigation where traditional newspapers might place edition information or section titles. The home and blog pages have the iconic columnar formatting most are accustomed to finding in newspapers. The teaching page has a single column for clarity of their sequential content. It mimics how one might find a recipe formatted in newspapers. The research page is a combination of both. It most closely aligns with the style of an advertisements page with dotted section borderings. It includes quotes from other people that make me think about the work I do, vaguely reminiscient of how early American newspaper editors would copy stories from other sources into their own to be republished in a scissors-and-paste fashion.

Print

Despite the aesthetic similarities, part of the historical charm associated with newspapers is the materiality. The textual content can be downloaded for print using the button in the footer! It is generated by a plugin each time I build the site. While I considered creating an RSS feed as a modern alternative for blog posts, I decided against it due to the diverse nature of my interests and variable formats of my site content. That might change as my writing habits evolve, but my different media channels are the best ways to get updates for now.
